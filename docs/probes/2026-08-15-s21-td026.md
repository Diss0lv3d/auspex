## 2. What was actually wrong

Two mechanisms, both confirmed by execution before anything was changed:

1. **RRF fused chunks, and the answer is a page.** T1566's three chunks sat at
   neural ranks 2/4/5 and each scored separately, while single-chunk T1598
   accumulated across both legs. Fixed by `rrf_fuse_docs` — one rank per
   document per leg. Deliberately *not* by summing a doc's chunks, which would
   invert the bias and let a long page beat a precise one.
2. **Neither leg had a signal for "this page IS the term."** BM25 rewards a body
   for repeating the query's words, which a sub-technique does more than its
   parent. Added in code (`auspex/kb/canonical.py`) as the question **are the
   title and the question about each other?**

### The one-sided version was wrong, and the corpus said so

Title coverage alone ("how much of the title did the question ask for?") took
the battery to 6/7 and then walked into the same class of error from the other
side: asked *what is credential dumping*, ATT&CK offers a page titled simply
**Credentials** (T1589.001 — harvesting victim identity information, a
reconnaissance page). Its title is 100% asked-for. `title_score` is the harmonic
mean of that measure and its mirror ("how much of the question does the title
account for?"), where "Credentials" accounts for one word and "OS Credential
Dumping" for two. **Each measure alone was observed picking a wrong page.**

### Depth mattered as much as ranking

T1003 was above its floor at fused rank **9** — outside a top-5 cut, so nothing
could promote it. The definition path now fuses over 12 candidates and truncates
to `top_k` *after* promotion. One wider fetch, no extra round trip.

## 3. Why this cannot fabricate

Promotion re-orders a list that has **already cleared the neural floor** and
never adds to it. The off-corpus half of the battery is unchanged at 4/4, and
`test_promotion_cannot_resurrect_an_off_corpus_miss` pins the property against
an empty neural leg with a BM25 hit waiting to be wrongly promoted.

## 5. What the battery does NOT measure (found while live-verifying)

**Only 5 of the 7 positives reach the definition path in production at all.**
The battery drives `search_definition()` directly; the *classifier* decides which
questions go there, and it routes two of them elsewhere:

- `how do adversaries abuse command line interpreters` → **deep** (LLM)
- `living off the land binaries` → **deep** (LLM)

Three of the four off-corpus queries are likewise `deep`; only *what is quantum
entanglement* classifies as a definition, and that is the one whose honest miss
is verified above. This is not a defect — the 92/8 split is D-006 code and
"living off the land binaries" is not phrased as a definition question — but it
means the battery is an acceptance test **for the retrieval function**, which is
what changed, and not a measurement of end-user answers. Said out loud here
because the README now cites its numbers.
